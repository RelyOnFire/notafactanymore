For nearly two centuries, king cobras across a vast range from the Indian subcontinent to Southeast Asia were generally placed in a single species, *Ophiophagus hannah*. Regional differences were known, but they had not produced a formal species-level split.

Genetic work changed the picture by identifying four deeply separated geographic lineages. The 2024 revision then examined 153 whole or skeletal specimens, compared diagnostic anatomy and revisited the historical names attached to the group. It restricted *O. hannah* to one lineage, revived *O. bungarus*, and described *O. kaalinga* and *O. salvatana*.

The animals did not suddenly become different in 2024. Taxonomy changed because the accumulated genetic and morphological evidence supported recognizing evolutionary lineages that had previously been grouped under one name.
