Soft corals are rich in unusual terpenes, a large family of natural products that includes defensive chemicals and compounds studied for possible medical use. For years, one likely source was the coral microbiome. Many sessile marine animals obtain complex natural products from bacteria or other symbionts, and the enzymes needed to build the coral compounds had been difficult to find in animal genomes.

Two independent studies changed that picture in 2022.

One group identified a lineage of terpene-cyclase genes encoded by octocorals themselves. The enzymes could build the carbon skeletons of characteristic coral terpenes. A second group found coral biosynthetic gene clusters that produced precursors to eunicellane- and cembrane-type compounds, including pathways related to molecules such as eleutherobin.

Microbes associated with corals still make biologically active compounds, and the coral holobiont remains chemically complex. The correction is that a microbial producer is not required to explain many of the signature terpenes.

The animal host has its own ancient terpene-making machinery.
