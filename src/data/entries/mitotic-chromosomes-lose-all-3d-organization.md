During interphase, chromosomes form large-scale compartments, topologically associating domains, loops, and smaller regulatory contacts. Hi-C studies showed that much of this organization disappears when chromosomes condense for mitosis, supporting the idea that cells largely rebuild their 3D genome architecture after division.

Higher-resolution mapping published in 2025 showed that the reset is not complete. Using region-capture Micro-C in mouse erythroblasts, researchers detected small regulatory microcompartments in prometaphase. These interactions became stronger through anaphase and telophase before weakening again in G1.

The result does not overturn earlier Hi-C work. A/B compartments, TADs, and many CTCF/cohesin loops really do disappear during mitosis. The new method resolved a much smaller class of contacts that earlier maps could not see.

The claim therefore narrows from “mitosis erases higher-order genome organization” to “mitosis erases much of the large-scale organization.” Some fine-scale regulatory interactions can persist through chromosome division and may help carry regulatory relationships into the next cell cycle.
