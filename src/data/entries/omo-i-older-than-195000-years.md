Omo I, a *Homo sapiens* fossil from Ethiopia's Omo-Kibish Formation, became an important reference point for the early history of our species. In 2005, improved dating of volcanic material led researchers to a preferred age of about 195,000 years.

That estimate was widely repeated, although the volcanic layers around the fossils remained difficult to correlate precisely.

A 2022 study revisited the ash. Researchers used the chemical fingerprint of volcanic glass to match a thick ash layer above Omo I to a major eruption of Shala volcano. Dating of that eruption placed it at about 233,000 years ago, with an uncertainty of roughly 22,000 years.

Because the ash lies above the fossil-bearing sediments, Omo I must be older than the eruption. The new number therefore does not mean that the fossil itself is exactly 233,000 years old. It establishes a substantially older minimum age.

The correction moved Omo I beyond the familiar ~195,000-year estimate and widened the time depth of early *Homo sapiens* in eastern Africa.
