C4 photosynthesis and crassulacean acid metabolism, or CAM, solve a similar problem in different ways. Both concentrate carbon dioxide around the enzyme Rubisco, but C4 plants mainly separate parts of the process between different cells, while CAM plants separate them by time of day.

That difference led to the idea that the two systems could not operate in the same cells. Purslane plants were an awkward exception because they were known to use both C4 and CAM, but the prevailing explanation put the two processes in different parts of the leaf.

In 2022, researchers mapped gene activity across the leaves of common purslane, *Portulaca oleracea*. They found that the C4 and CAM systems were not segregated as expected. Both operated in the same photosynthetic cells, and the data suggested that metabolites produced by CAM could feed directly into the C4 cycle.

A separate genomic and expression study published the same year also found that the two programs were deeply integrated.

C4 and CAM still rarely occur together. What changed was the proposed reason: they are not inherently incompatible inside the same cells.
