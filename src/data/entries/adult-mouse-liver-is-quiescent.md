The adult liver is famous for regeneration. Remove part of it or damage it chemically, and normally quiet hepatocytes can re-enter the cell cycle and rebuild lost mass.

That dramatic injury response helped shape a simple picture of the healthy adult liver: under ordinary conditions, hepatocytes were largely quiescent and significant proliferation began when tissue was damaged or removed.

A 2023 study found a physiological route into proliferation without liver injury. When healthy adult mice were switched from unrestricted feeding to an intermittent-fasting schedule, hepatocytes began dividing rapidly during fasting and refeeding.

The response depended on two signals working together. FGF15 provided a systemic nutrient-related signal, while local WNT signalling helped determine which hepatocytes completed division. The resulting proliferation restored the characteristic ratio between liver mass and body mass as body weight changed.

The result is specific to the mouse model and the feeding regimen that was tested. It does not show that healthy human livers are constantly proliferating. It does show that adult hepatocyte division is not reserved for injury: normal changes in nutrient state can also activate a regenerative programme.
