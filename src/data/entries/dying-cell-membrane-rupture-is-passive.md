Many forms of lytic cell death end with a swollen cell losing its plasma membrane and releasing intracellular material. The conventional explanation treated this last rupture as passive: ion imbalance draws in water, osmotic pressure rises, and the membrane eventually fails.

A 2021 genetic screen showed that the final step has its own molecular machinery. Cells lacking the membrane protein NINJ1 could still undergo upstream death programs such as pyroptosis, necrosis, and apoptosis, but many remained ballooned and physically intact instead of releasing large intracellular proteins. Death and membrane rupture could therefore be separated.

Later structural work showed how NINJ1 can execute the rupture. The protein polymerizes into filaments at damaged membrane edges, helping stabilize and enlarge lesions until the membrane breaks apart.

Osmotic swelling still matters, and not every kind of membrane damage must use NINJ1. The overturned part is the assumption that terminal rupture in major lytic death pathways is merely a passive mechanical consequence. In many cases it is an actively controlled final stage of cell death.
