The restriction point became one of the standard ideas used to explain how animal cells decide whether to divide. In the classical model, a cell depended on external growth signals during G1. Once it crossed the restriction point, internal feedback was expected to carry the cycle forward even if those signals disappeared.

That idea was often summarized as a point of no return. Textbooks described the G1 checkpoint as the stage at which a cell irreversibly commits to division.

Experiments published in 2023 tested that commitment more directly. Researchers tracked individual mammalian cells while removing growth signals or blocking CDK4/6, enzymes that help drive cells through the cycle. Some cells that had already entered S phase, where DNA is copied, or had progressed into G2 still reversed course and exited the cycle.

The results do not mean that the restriction-point concept has no value. Earlier work had already shown that real cell-cycle commitment is more complicated than a single switch. What changed in 2023 was the strongest version of the textbook model: entry into S phase did not guarantee that a cell would continue to mitosis.

Cell-cycle commitment is therefore better understood as a process that can remain sensitive to growth signalling well beyond the classical G1 boundary.
